MAVEC: A Dependency-Aware Multi-Agent Framework for Visual Evidence Composition in Mechanical Engineering Drawings

Anonymous Authors
Under Review

Abstract

Mechanical engineering drawings are essential to engineering workflows such as design review and assembly verification, yet understanding them requires compositional reasoning over visual evidence distributed across multiple views and annotations. We propose MAVEC, a dependency-aware multi-agent framework that coordinates specialized agents to progressively acquire and connect visual evidence. The acquired observations are organized into a Visual Evidence Graph (VEG), which preserves grounded claims and their dependencies to support deterministic reasoning. Experiments on the FORGE benchmark demonstrate that MAVEC achieves 91.47% Overall accuracy and 83.90% Hard accuracy, outperforming all evaluated baselines. Further analyses show that selected visual evidence substantially improves source-grounded claim recovery over random crops, demonstrating the utility of evidence-centric collaboration for engineering drawing understanding.

MAVEC

Overview of MAVEC. A Planner generates a semantic reasoning program, a deterministic Controller coordinates specialized agents to acquire visual evidence, and the evidence is organized into a shared Visual Evidence Graph for deterministic answer computation.

A Planner turns the question into a semantic reasoning program that specifies the operations and the evidence they need, without reading values from the drawing itself. A deterministic Controller then checks which operands are still unresolved and dispatches requests to three role-specialized agents sharing one MLLM: a Locator that finds relevant regions, a Reader that extracts dimensions and BOM values, and a Linker that connects entities such as features, markers and BOM rows. Each observation is validated and added to the Visual Evidence Graph as a typed claim with its visual evidence, provenance and dependencies, so that later requests are driven by what has already been found. Once the graph is complete, a deterministic Executor runs the program over it to derive the answer. The agents are trained with role-conditioned SFT on structured tool outputs, never on final answers.

Results

Accuracy (%) on the FORGE test set. Standard evaluates familiar drawing reasoning, while Hard includes four compositional reasoning families (C1–C4).

With the same Qwen3-VL-4B backbone, MAVEC achieves the highest Overall (91.47%) and Hard (83.90%) accuracy. Single-agent post-training reaches strong Standard accuracy but stays below 41% on Hard, while multi-agent approaches lift Hard performance and MAVEC improves further, with the best or tied-best accuracy on every Hard family, including 92.13% on C2 and 94.83% on C3. Conditional filtering in C4 remains the most challenging at 58.06%.

Standard. MAVEC follows the marked feature F01 through its component marker and BOM entry to identify the corresponding component.

C1: dimension comparison and component identification. MAVEC compares the displayed diameters of two marked features and follows the ownership relation of the selected feature to obtain the component.

C2: dimension comparison followed by quantity retrieval. MAVEC selects the feature with the larger displayed diameter, resolves its component and BOM reference, and retrieves the assembly quantity.

C3: comparison across component–quantity chains. MAVEC resolves the component and quantity associated with each marked feature, then selects the feature belonging to the more frequently used component.

C4: conditional filtering and diameter comparison. MAVEC identifies the candidate features, filters them by component quantity, and compares the remaining diameters to select the final feature.
