MAVEC: A Dependency-Aware Multi-Agent Framework for Visual Evidence Composition in Mechanical Engineering Drawings

Anonymous Authors
Under Review
Paper

MAVEC combines drawing-level context with localized evidence to recover feature dimensions, component associations and BOM quantities, producing an answer that can be traced back to the original drawing.

Abstract

Engineering drawing review requires accurate interpretation of visual information and traceable evidence that engineers can use to verify conclusions. However, relevant information is often distributed across drawing regions, requiring interdependent evidence acquisition and reasoning. We present MAVEC, a dependency-aware multi-agent framework designed to support evidence-grounded engineering drawing review. Specialized agents progressively locate, extract, and link visual information, guided by previously acquired evidence. MAVEC organizes these observations into a Visual Evidence Graph (VEG), preserving visual provenance and acquisition dependencies for deterministic reasoning. On the FORGE benchmark, MAVEC achieves 91.47% Overall and 83.90% Hard accuracy, outperforming all evaluated baselines. By connecting computed answers to their supporting visual evidence, MAVEC provides an inspectable reasoning process to assist engineers in reviewing technical drawings.

MAVEC

Overview of MAVEC. A Planner generates a structured reasoning plan, a deterministic Controller coordinates specialized agents to acquire visual evidence, and the evidence is organized into a shared Visual Evidence Graph for deterministic answer computation.

A Planner turns the question into a structured reasoning plan that specifies the operations and the evidence they need, without reading values from the drawing itself. A deterministic Controller then checks which operands are still unresolved and dispatches requests to three role-specialized agents sharing one MLLM: a Locator that finds relevant regions, a Reader that extracts dimensions and BOM values, and a Linker that connects entities such as features, markers and BOM rows. Each observation is validated and added to the Visual Evidence Graph as a typed claim with its visual evidence, provenance and dependencies, so that later requests are driven by what has already been found. Once the graph is complete, a deterministic Executor runs the planned operations over it to derive an answer traceable to the drawing, with an explicit fallback when the required evidence remains incomplete. The agents are trained with role-conditioned SFT on structured tool outputs, never on final answers.

Results

Accuracy (%) on the FORGE test set. Standard evaluates familiar drawing reasoning, while Hard includes four compositional reasoning families (C1–C4).

With the same Qwen3-VL-4B backbone, MAVEC achieves the highest Overall (91.47%) and Hard (83.90%) accuracy. Single-agent post-training reaches strong Standard accuracy but stays below 41% on Hard, while multi-agent approaches lift Hard performance and MAVEC improves further, with the best or tied-best accuracy on every Hard family, including 92.13% on C2 and 94.83% on C3. Conditional filtering in C4 remains the most challenging at 58.06%.

Evidence Analysis

We test whether the evidence MAVEC acquires is actually used and actually correct. Replaying the recorded evidence requests while removing, replacing or masking the selected crop lowers Overall accuracy by 1.17–1.58%p and Hard accuracy by 1.88–2.63%p, so the localized evidence supports the final predictions. Re-extracting 200 atomic claims recorded in the VEG, selected crops recover 70.50% of them versus 22.00% for random crops, and combining them with the drawing overview reaches 95.00%, showing that the selected regions contain the engineering facts the answer relies on.

Standard. MAVEC follows the marked feature F01 through its component marker and BOM entry to identify the corresponding component.

C1: dimension comparison and component identification. MAVEC compares the displayed diameters of two marked features and follows the ownership relation of the selected feature to obtain the component.

C2: dimension comparison followed by quantity retrieval. MAVEC selects the feature with the larger displayed diameter, resolves its component and BOM reference, and retrieves the assembly quantity.

C3: comparison across component–quantity chains. MAVEC resolves the component and quantity associated with each marked feature, then selects the feature belonging to the more frequently used component.

C4: conditional filtering and diameter comparison. MAVEC identifies the candidate features, filters them by component quantity, and compares the remaining diameters to select the final feature.
